Experiment 1 of 4 · illumination

The flashlight test

Stand where the photo was taken and switch on a flashlight as wide as the camera's view. What it touches is measured. Everything behind you, outside the beam, or in the shadow of furniture was never in the photo: it is imagined. That is an illumination map. Applied to a generated 3D world, this site calls the result a trust map, computed from an illumination score.

What you will learn

How to tell which parts of a generated 3D world a photo actually constrains, using an interactive toy.

Key takeaway

A single photo directly constrains only part of a 3D world; the rest is filled in by the model's learned prior. A seismic-style score for this is practically equal to plain visibility.

Try it

A 2D room with two pieces of furniture. Drag the camera, change its direction and field of view, and add a second photo. The dots are points on the walls and furniture, coloured by how well the photos constrain them.

Two ways to add a photo, two different gainsStepping sideways gives parallax, which measures distance for nearby points but barely adds coverage. Turning on the spot adds coverage but no parallax at all. A 360° panorama or Marble's multi-image mode is the second kind. You need both to get both.

Why one photo cannot fix distance

Does the maths beat plain visibility?

To test an uncertainty score you need a known answer. We use a practice exam, as geophysicists do with synthetic Earth models: a public example 3D world (a splat file published for download) plays the real room, we photograph it, a stand-in generator (a public depth network plus a room-box prior) rebuilds it, and we grade every splat against the truth. AUROC asks: pick one badly wrong splat and one good one at random; how often does the score call the wrong one less trustworthy? 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

Honest readingThe illumination score clearly beats the "inside the photo frame?" test, and differs from "does any ray reach it?" (visibility) by only about 0.003 AUROC: detectable over twelve cases, negligible in practice. The value of the seismic framing here is conceptual: the tiers, the depth-versus-appearance split, and the survey-design thinking. It is not a better score.
Where these numbers come from, and the caveats

Reproduced from the migrated code (experiments/wp0_baseline/reproduce.py) and matching the earlier recorded run to four decimals.

  • The generator is a stand-in, not Marble. "Imagined means wrong" is partly built in because the filled-in geometry is our own box prior.
  • Twelve cases come from three truth worlds and four yaw angles each, so they are not independent.
