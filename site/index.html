What could a geophysicist contribute to spatial intelligence and physical AI?

Companies such as World Labs build models that generate and reconstruct 3D worlds for people and robots. Seismic imaging has spent decades on a closely related problem: recovering a hidden 3D structure from sparse, indirect measurements, and saying honestly what the data do and do not tell you. This site explores how those methods could help, tries the ideas on small experiments, and checks what already exists.

This project starts from open problems that World Labs describes on its own public pages (for example, as its taxonomy post puts it, that “AI-generated geometry can look correct while containing self-intersections or wrong scale that produce nonsensical physics”, or that “Object shape, weight, and friction may be uncertain”) and asks whether ideas from geophysics could complement that work. It uses public information and small stand-in experiments only, and does not evaluate or compare any company's products. Much of the relevant prior work comes from the vision and robotics communities, which the literature page credits. The ideas are hypotheses to examine, and comments or corrections from World Labs or anyone else are welcome.

    The question

    Which seismic-imaging ideas (illumination and resolution, tomography, migration, multiples, full-waveform inversion, uncertainty) could improve how generated 3D worlds are built, trusted and used by robots?

    The short answer so far

    Almost every idea tried here already has verified prior work. What a geophysicist brings is mostly discipline: saying what the data constrain, testing against known truth, calibrating, and reporting negative results.

    What this site does not claim

    No new algorithm.Second-order optimisation, Fisher-information uncertainty, optimal-transport registration, shape-from-shadows and migration for non-line-of-sight imaging all already exist. The literature map lists them.
    Nothing here tests Marble itself.Testing real Marble output needs paid API credits and, first, World Labs' agreement that this research use is acceptable.
    Proofs of concept, not products.Small samples, a stand-in generator and idealised simulations. The Method page lists what each result does and does not show.
