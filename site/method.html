Method, rigour and reproduction

How this was done, and how to check it

Each research question was pre-specified with a numeric success criterion, compared against the strongest simple baseline, and reported with confidence intervals, including when it fails. Extra analyses are labelled exploratory.

What you will learn

How the experiments were run, what the results do and do not show, a glossary of terms, and what is and is not permitted or claimed.

Key takeaway

Criteria written down before each run, strong baselines, honest negative results; but a stand-in generator and small samples.

Research commitments

Pre-specification

Hypothesis, baselines, metrics and a numeric success criterion are written in prereg/ before each run, with a locally recorded hash and timestamp. The files were not committed beforehand, so the record is self-attested, not independently verifiable. Changes go in dated amendments.

Strong baselines first

Our own illumination score came out practically equal to plain visibility, so every later idea must beat the simple baseline, not a straw man.

Held-out by world

In the alignment experiment, hyper-parameters were tuned on one world and tested on two others. the robot and shadows experiments have no held-out split. Scores are not picked after seeing test results, and post-hoc changes are labelled.

Statistics

Paired bootstrap over cases. A "win" needs an interval that excludes zero, and the effect size has to matter too.

Negative results published

A success criterion that is not met is still a result. The robot benchmark is reported as inconclusive, with its design flaws listed.

Circularity check

Each experiment states which parts of the "truth" are built in, and reports the fair-test numbers separately.

Public information only

Everything here is built from public pages, public papers and public example files. The starting points are open problems that World Labs describes on its own pages; the ideas are offered as complementary, and nothing evaluates or compares any company's products.

    Glossary

    TermPlain meaning
    Gaussian splatA soft, semi-transparent, coloured blob. A splat world can contain hundreds of thousands to millions of them.
    IlluminationHow strongly the photo constrains a splat; the diagonal of JᵀJ, as in seismic imaging.
    AUROCChance that the score ranks a randomly chosen wrong splat as less trustworthy than a good one. 0.5 is a coin flip.
    Fisher informationHow much the data change if a parameter moves; its inverse bounds how well the parameter can be estimated.
    Null directionA change to the model that leaves the data unchanged. Near-and-small versus far-and-large is one.
    Convergence basinHow wrong the starting guess can be before the optimiser stops finding the right answer.
    Stand-in generatorOur own simple world generator used as a controlled test bed. It is not Marble.

venv/bin/python -m pytest -q

    Limits and permissions

    Not MarbleAll numbers use a stand-in generator. Testing real Marble output (the planned ensemble study) needs paid API credits and, first, written confirmation from World Labs that this research use is acceptable under their terms.
    No third-party assetsWorld Labs and Spark files are not redistributed. The data script downloads public example files from their public locations; check World Labs' terms before reusing them. Every image on this site is this project's own plot, schematic or render.
