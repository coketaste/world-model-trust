The evidence

Four small experiments

The ideas on the previous pages are cheap to state and easy to over-believe. So four of them were tried on small, controlled tests where the right answer is known. They are proofs of concept on a stand-in generator, not tests of Marble, and each result is reported whichever way it went.

What you will find

One card per experiment: the question in plain words, how it was tested, and what came out, with a link to the full page.

Key takeaway

One idea was practically a tie with a simple baseline, two were inconclusive, and one worked only in an idealised simulation. That is useful: it shows where to look next.

How to read these results

A stand-in generator, not Marble

The "generated world" is built by a public depth network plus a simple room-box rule. It lets us know the truth. It says nothing about how Marble itself behaves; testing that needs paid access and permission.

Known truth, held out where possible

A public example world plays the real room. Scores are graded against it. The alignment study tuned on one world and tested on two others; the other experiments have no held-out split.

What "pre-specified" means here

Each question's hypothesis and success criterion were written down before its run, with a locally recorded hash. They were not committed beforehand, so the record is self-attested. Changes are listed as dated amendments, and extra analyses are labelled exploratory.

Small samples

Twelve cases from three worlds is a proof of concept. Intervals are indicative, and cases from the same world are not independent.
