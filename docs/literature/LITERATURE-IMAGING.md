# Verified literature map: seismic imaging methods and their existing counterparts in vision, imaging and robotics

> A literature map. It is not an assessment of any company's products.

Prepared 2026-10-03 for the world-model-trust project. Purpose: to avoid misrepresenting what already exists, and to locate where a seismic-imaging background could plausibly contribute. It is not a claim of priority by anyone, and it takes no position against any group's work.

## 1. How to read this
- Rows pair a seismic concept with its closest existing counterparts. **Relationship** says whether the link is the same mathematics, an analogy, or a documented direct adaptation. **Status** says whether the transfer looks done, partly done or open (in this search only).
- Reference keys: `G` = geophysics / wave imaging (including ML-for-FWI), `V` = vision, graphics, computational imaging, robotics. Every key is listed in section 5 with its verification status.
- Evidence labels: **VERIFIED** means metadata (first author, year, title, identifier) was fetched and matched. **PARTIAL** means a related version was verified but not the exact item, or a claim detail was not checked. **UNVERIFIED** means not confirmed; such items are not cited as support.
- Statements marked *opinion*, *analogy* or *hypothesis* are mine, not documented findings.

### Method and limits
- No general web search was available. References were checked with the arXiv API and arXiv abstract pages, the Crossref API, and the Semantic Scholar API (for one abstract). Abstracts were read for the entries whose claims are quoted; most other entries were verified at title/first-author/year level only.
- Author columns give only the **first author** as returned by the APIs; co-authors were not verified and are omitted on purpose.
- Several DOIs that were first noted from recollection were wrong when checked (two resolved to unrelated papers) and were replaced by searched-and-verified ones. A DOI listed here has been fetched.
- 'Not found in this search' is not evidence of absence. Conference-only, industry and non-English work may be missed. Dates in 2026 are as returned by arXiv.
- Seismic textbook results (e.g. standard FWI/migration theory) are cited at paper level; no claim here depends on reading full papers.

## 3. Things geophysics has that vision may lack or under-use (hypotheses)
These are opinions or hypotheses, each with the nearest documented hook. None is a finding.
1. **Resolution analysis as a deliverable.** Seismic practice reports resolution and illumination with the image [G19][G22][G23]. Vision work reports diagonal Fisher scores [V28][V30] more often than point-spread functions. *Opinion.*
2. **Blind-test and benchmark culture.** Marmousi-style synthetic benchmarks [G35] and OpenFWI [G40] exist in geophysics. Generated-world evaluation exists [V52]; whether it reports per-region trust against ground-truth geometry was not re-verified here.
3. **Target-oriented imaging.** Hessians defined for a target region [G21] map to task-specific uncertainty (e.g. the floor along a robot route). *Hypothesis.*
4. **Model-space versus data-space uncertainty.** The distinction is standard in Bayesian FWI [G30][G33] and is relevant to the finding in [V46] that global variance does not localise in-object error. *Hypothesis.*
5. **Joint inversion and structural coupling.** Coupling parameters through shared structure is routine in geophysics; the analogue (coupling geometry, appearance and material) is plausible but unchecked. *Opinion.*
6. **Large-adjoint engineering.** Checkpointing and domain decomposition for adjoint wavefields [G11] may inform very large differentiable simulators. *Hypothesis.*
